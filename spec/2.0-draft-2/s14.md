# 14. VC Evidence Profile

## 14.1 Scope

This profile defines entries whose content is a complete W3C Verifiable Credential signed by its issuer, held as an evidence item (4.2.2). It is identified as https://hcp.example/spec/profiles/vc-evidence/2.0 (2.7), and the same identifier is the value of `content.profile` for such an entry. It adds to any package (18) and replaces v1's VC-Extended conformance level.

An implementation conforming to this profile MUST support such entries as specified in this section, with the signature boundary observed (4.2.2).

## 14.2 Credential and Signature Boundary

The credential is held whole in the evidence item whose `role` is `content`: its own `@context`, `type`, `issuer`, `credentialSubject`, and either an embedded Data Integrity proof or the EnvelopedVerifiableCredential form, per 14.5. The credential is a self-contained artifact, verifiable by a generic Verifiable Credentials verifier without reference to the entry around it and without knowledge of HCP vocabulary.

    "content": {
      "profile": "https://hcp.example/spec/profiles/vc-evidence/2.0",
      "evidence": 0
    }

- `content.evidence` is the index in `evidence` of the item holding the credential. `content` carries no `value`: the credential is not restated.
- The credential's proof covers the credential and nothing else (4.2.2). Lifecycle transitions --- a status change under 4.7, a category change under 4.2.1 --- do not invalidate the issuer's proof.
- The credential carries no HCP vocabulary. It does not reference the HCP JSON-LD context, and it carries neither category nor declaration type. The issuer asserts; the HCP Provider classifies that assertion in `classification` and `assertion` when it ingests it (4.2). An issuer needs no knowledge of this specification in order to produce a credential an HCP instance can hold.
- The credential's `credentialSubject.id` MUST identify the same Context Subject as the entry's `subject.id`.
- The credential's dates are its own, set by its issuer: `validFrom` and `validUntil` under the Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0. The entry's `assertion.validUntil` MUST NOT be later than the credential's `validUntil` where the credential has one.
- A credential keeps the `@context` its issuer signed it with. The HCP Provider MUST NOT substitute another version of a context, including a newer version of the same context, because doing so invalidates the proof.

## 14.3 Roles

The Verifiable Credentials roles are defined here and are not Core roles (3.2.6).

| VC role | In HCP |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Issuer | The Context Asserter of an entry whose content is a credential. `record.provenance.assertedBy.id` identifies it (3.5). |
| Credential subject | The Context Subject. The credential's `credentialSubject` identifies them, as the entry's `subject` does (14.2). |
| Holder | The Context Subject, whose HCP instance holds the credential. The HCP Provider stores and returns it on their behalf. |
| Verifier | The HCP Provider, at ingest (14.6) and before each disclosure (14.7). A Context Client that receives an entry carrying a credential MAY verify it independently. |

## 14.5 Securing Credentials

A credential that is an entry's content MUST be secured by one of the following:

| Mechanism | `vc_securing` value | Specification | Verify | Notes |
|----------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|--------------|------------------------------------------|
| Embedded Data Integrity proof | `eddsa-rdfc-2022` | W3C VC Data Integrity EdDSA Cryptosuites | MUST | Requires RDF canonicalization, confined to the credential (4.1.1). Broadest support in current education wallets and verifiers. |
| Embedded Data Integrity proof, selective disclosure | `ecdsa-sd-2023` | W3C VC Data Integrity ECDSA Cryptosuites | SHOULD | Requires RDF canonicalization. A holder can derive a proof revealing only some claims (14.9). |
| Enveloping proof | `vc-jose-cose` | W3C VC-JOSE-COSE | MAY | JOSE, SD-JWT, or COSE securing. No canonicalization. SD-JWT securing provides selective disclosure (14.9). |

Implementations conforming to this profile MUST declare which mechanisms they issue and which they verify, in the discovery document as `vc_securing` (19).

An implementation MUST verify `eddsa-rdfc-2022`, SHOULD verify `ecdsa-sd-2023`, and MAY verify VC-JOSE-COSE. Two conformant implementations therefore always share one mechanism, and `verifies` always includes `eddsa-rdfc-2022`. An issuer may secure a credential with any mechanism in the table, and the declaration in the discovery document is what allows it to determine whether its credentials will be accepted. A content credential secured with a mechanism the HCP Provider does not verify is rejected as `credential-invalid` (14.6); a supporting credential is kept and marked as failed (14.8).

The exact serialization of an enveloping proof follows VC-JOSE-COSE and the EnvelopedVerifiableCredential form defined in the VC Data Model; this specification does not restate it.

An issuer's signature secures the integrity of the credential; it does not itself authorize its disclosure (3.11, invariant 11). Whether an HCP Provider may release an entry carrying a credential to a given HCP Client is governed entirely by the grant model (6) --- a valid proof establishes that the credential is what it claims to be, not that any particular client is entitled to see it.

## 14.6 Ingest

- Cryptographic proofs MUST be validated on ingest. An entry whose content credential fails validation MUST be rejected with credential invalid (5.5) rather than stored unverified.
- A content credential fails validation where its proof does not verify, where it is secured with a mechanism the HCP Provider does not verify (14.5), where the verification method cannot be obtained, or where its status shows it revoked or suspended (14.7). The error's detail names which, and does not reveal the credential's position in a status list.
- Where the issuer is identified by a DID, the HCP Provider MUST resolve it to obtain the verification method used to validate the proof.
- A write MAY omit `assertedBy.id`. The HCP Provider MUST set it from the credential's `issuer`. Where it is supplied and differs, the write is rejected with invalid request.
- An entry whose content credential the HCP Provider has validated is recorded with assurance `cryptographically-verified` (3.6).
- The HCP Provider records the outcome on each evidence item it verifies, as `verification` (4.2.2).
- An entry whose credential's `validUntil` has passed is `expired` (4.2.4). It is not disclosed to a Context Client, and it remains visible to the Context Subject.
- A write carrying a credential as its content is `issuer-attested` and is accepted only from an HCP Client authenticated as the issuer, or authorized by it (3.7).

A credential that supports a statement is verified on ingest too, under 14.8, but a failure does not reject the entry.

## 14.7 Credential Status

A credential whose issuer can revoke or suspend it names a status entry in `credentialStatus`. The HCP Provider checks it as follows:

- **Mechanism.** An implementation conforming to this profile MUST support Bitstring Status List v1.0: a `credentialStatus` of type `BitstringStatusListEntry`, with `statusPurpose` `revocation` or `suspension`. The status list credential is itself a credential, and MUST be verified before it is relied on. A content credential naming only a status mechanism the HCP Provider does not support is rejected as `credential-invalid`, because its status cannot be kept.
- **At ingest.** A content credential that is revoked or suspended is rejected (14.6).
- **Before disclosure.** The HCP Provider MUST NOT disclose an entry whose content credential is revoked or suspended. It establishes status from a cached copy of the status list no older than its declared interval, `credential_status_interval` (19). The interval MUST NOT exceed 24 hours, or 1 hour under the Health Context Profile (16.2). Where it cannot establish status within the interval, it MUST NOT disclose the entry until it can; the entry's status does not change.
- **Resting states.** A revoked content credential makes the entry `revoked`, and a suspended one makes it `suspended` (4.2.4). Neither is read by a Context Client. A `revoked` entry leaves that state only by a revision carrying a new credential. A `suspended` entry returns to the state it held when the issuer lifts the suspension.
- **Audit.** The HCP Provider MUST append a `context.credentialStatus` event when it sets or lifts either state, and when it changes the `verification` of a supporting credential for a change of status (8.1).

The HCP Provider fetches and caches a status list whole. It MUST NOT fetch status in a way that identifies a credential to its issuer, or that is timed to a disclosure, so that an issuer does not learn when a person's credential is used. A credential without `credentialStatus` cannot be revoked by its issuer, and its expiry is its only end (4.2.4).

## 14.8 Supporting Credentials

A credential whose `role` is `supports` backs a statement in `content.value` (4.2.2). Under this profile:

- The HCP Provider MUST verify a supporting credential on ingest, as it verifies a content credential (14.6), and MUST record the outcome on the evidence item as `verification` (4.2.2).
- A supporting credential that fails is kept, with `verification.result` `failed`, and the entry is accepted. The entry's content is the asserter's statement, and a failed attachment does not void it.
- The HCP Provider re-establishes the status of a supporting credential under 14.7 and updates `verification` when it changes. A revoked or suspended supporting credential does not change the entry's status.
- `verification` is disclosed with the evidence item (7.4), so that a Context Client relying on a supporting credential knows what was checked and when.
- Verification never raises assurance and never confers standing (3.11, invariant 15). A `person-declared` entry with a verified supporting credential remains `person-declared`, with the assurance its asserter's identity established (3.6), and a failed credential does not lower it. Each consumer weighs the credential for itself.

The Health and Education Context Profiles require more of an `issuer-attested` entry's credential (16.2, 16.4).

## 14.9 Selective Disclosure

A credential secured with `ecdsa-sd-2023`, or with SD-JWT under VC-JOSE-COSE, lets its holder derive a presentation that reveals only some of its claims, verifiable against the issuer's original proof. Where an HCP Provider verifies such a mechanism (14.5):

- When it discloses an entry carrying such a credential to a Context Client, it SHOULD disclose a derived credential in its place, revealing the claims the request's purpose requires (7.1) and every claim the issuer made mandatory.
- The stored credential is unaltered (4.2.2). Derivation is a property of the disclosure, as minimization is (7.4).
- The evidence item disclosed lists the claims revealed, as JSON Pointers into the stored credential, in `revealed` (7.4). The `context.read` event records the same list for each entry (8.2).

A credential secured with `eddsa-rdfc-2022` or plain JOSE securing cannot be derived from, and is disclosed whole. A derived `ecdsa-sd-2023` presentation limits what is revealed, but two presentations of the same credential can still be linked to each other. How a grant names the claims it may receive, and unlinkable disclosure, are later work (26).
