# 17. Delegated Authorization Profile

## 17.1 Scope

This profile defines how a person other than the Context Subject acts as Authorizing Principal for them: a parent or guardian for a minor, a guardian or attorney-in-fact for an adult, a caregiver. It is identified as https://hcp.example/spec/profiles/delegated-authorization/2.0 (2.7). An implementation that issues a grant whose Authorizing Principal is not the Context Subject MUST conform to it (3.3, 18.1).

- A delegate is a natural person. An organization --- a school, a clinic, an employer --- is never an Authorizing Principal. It acts through HCP Clients under a grant that the Context Subject, or a delegate, authorized.
- A delegate cannot delegate further. No grant or delegation made under this profile makes any other party a delegate. Whether v2 permits delegation chains remains open (26.1).
- The Context Subject is always the person the entries describe (3.2.1). A delegation changes who may authorize grants for them, not whose context it is.

It absorbs v1 §7.3, multi-party and guardian consent.

## 17.2 Delegations

A delegation records one person's authority to act as Authorizing Principal for the Context Subject. The Authorization Service establishes it, and the HCP Provider holds its record as it holds grants (6.1.5).

    {
      "delegationId": "urn:uuid:{delegation-uuid}",
      "subject": { "id": "{subject identifier}" },
      "delegate": { "id": "{identifier}", "name": "{name the person can recognize}" },
      "relationship": "parent-or-guardian | legal-guardian | attorney-in-fact | caregiver | {URI}",
      "basis": {
        "method": "appointed | attested | evidence | unspecified",
        "description": "{what the authority rests on, shown to the person}",
        "evidence": [ ],
        "covers": ["{high-sensitivity category}"],
        "establishedBy": "{authorization-service-identifier}",
        "establishedAt": "{ISO-8601}"
      },
      "scope": {
        "categories": ["{category}"],
        "actions": ["read | write"]
      },
      "term": { "endsAt": "{ISO-8601 | null}", "atMajority": true },
      "ended": { "at": "{ISO-8601}", "reason": "majority | expiry | basis-ended | relinquished | ended-by-subject" }
    }

| Member | Rule |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `delegationId` | Stable for the life of the delegation. Named by every grant issued under it (17.4) and by audit events (8.2). |
| `subject` | The Context Subject |
| `delegate` | The delegate: a natural person, with a name the Context Subject can recognize |
| `relationship` | The delegate's relationship to the Context Subject. The registered values are listed in 25.1; any other is an absolute URI. It confers nothing by itself: what the delegate may do is set by `scope`. |
| `basis` | What the authority rests on, and how the Authorization Service established it (17.3) |
| `scope` | The categories and actions the delegate may authorize (17.4) |
| `term` | When the delegation ends: at `endsAt`, and at the Context Subject's age of majority where `atMajority` is true. The Authorization Service determines the age of majority under the law that applies. `endsAt` is null where no end is known. |
| `ended` | Absent while the delegation holds. Set when it ends, with the reason (17.6). |

- The Authorization Service establishes a delegation and records `basis`. The HCP Provider MUST NOT take `basis`, `scope` or `term` from a request by an HCP Client. It appends a `delegation.established` event when it records a delegation (8.1).
- `relationship` on a delegation is distinct from `subject.relationship` on an entry, which stays reserved and uninterpreted (11.3).
- The Context Subject and the delegate can each read the delegation, through a Management Client (18.4). Neither may change it; a change is a new delegation.

## 17.3 Basis of Authority

| `basis.method` | Meaning |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `appointed` | The Context Subject appointed the delegate themselves, at the Authorization Service, through a Management Client acting for them. The Context Subject can end the delegation at any time (17.6) |
| `attested` | The Authorization Service established the relationship by means outside HCP --- an account relationship, an enrollment process, an identity check --- and attests it |
| `evidence` | Evidence was presented and is recorded in `basis.evidence`: a credential of guardianship, a court order, a power of attorney, an enrollment record |
| `unspecified` | The basis is not known. Used only for a delegation converted from v1 (17.8) |

- Evidence that is a Verifiable Credential is held whole, as an evidence item is (4.2.2). Where the implementation conforms to the VC Evidence Profile, it is verified and its status kept as for a supporting credential (14.8).
- The basis records how authority was established. It does not require strong evidence, and it is not a judgment of the delegate's fitness to hold the authority, which belongs to the law and the deployment.
- A basis that was not established MUST NOT be recorded as established, and an `unspecified` basis MUST NOT be presented to anyone as `attested` (3.11, invariant 15).
- The Context Subject and the delegate are shown `relationship`, `basis.method` and `basis.description`.

## 17.4 Scope and Grants Under a Delegation

**Scope.** A delegation's scope lists the categories and actions the delegate may authorize. It names each category; there is no wildcard.

- A high-sensitivity category (4.5), including any custom category (7.6), MAY be in scope only where it is listed in `basis.covers`, recording that the basis extends to it. A parent's authority over a minor's learning context does not by itself extend to their `health_context`.
- A grant authorized by the delegate MUST NOT reach a category or action outside the delegation's scope, or one a standing rule denies (6.3). A request for one is refused at issuance. A delegate may set standing rules within the delegation's scope, which the Context Subject can remove (6.3).
- Governance operations (5.4) performed by a delegate, through a Management Client acting for them, reach only entries in categories within scope.

**Grants.** A grant authorized by a delegate is an ordinary grant (6.1.5), with these differences:

    "authorizingPrincipal": {
      "id": "{identifier of the delegate, specific to the HCP Client}",
      "delegation": "urn:uuid:{delegation-uuid}"
    }

- `authorizingPrincipal` identifies the delegate, by an identifier specific to the HCP Client, and `delegation` names the delegation. Where the Context Subject authorizes a grant themselves, `delegation` is absent.
- Before the approving act (6.1.6), the delegate MUST also be told that they are authorizing on the Context Subject's behalf, and when the delegation ends.
- A temporary grant's `expiresAt` MUST NOT be later than the delegation's `term.endsAt`. Every grant under a delegation ends when the delegation ends (17.6).
- The `grant.issued` event names the delegation (8.2).
- A Context Client reading its own grant (5.2.6) is not shown `delegation`. The delegation's identifier is the same in every grant made under it, and showing it would let two clients link the person.

## 17.5 The Context Subject's Override

- The Context Subject MUST be able to revoke any grant made for them, including every grant a delegate authorized, at any time, through a Management Client or the person-facing capabilities required by 18. Revocation by the Context Subject MUST NOT be conditioned on the delegate's agreement, and is never rate limited (6.2.1).
- The delegate is told of the revocation. A new grant for the same client is a new approving act, recorded as `grant.issued`, and the Context Subject sees it.
- A delegate MAY revoke any grant within the delegation's scope.
- Where the delegation's `basis.method` is `appointed`, the Context Subject MUST be able to end the delegation itself at any time, through a Management Client (17.6). A delegation resting on any other basis --- a court order, a parent's authority, an attested relationship --- is ended only as 17.6 sets out, though its grants can always be revoked.
- Whether the Context Subject may also authorize grants while a delegation holds, and from what age, is set by the deployment under the law that applies to it. A grant the Context Subject authorizes is a Core grant, whether or not a delegation holds.
- The Context Subject MUST be able to read their whole audit log at any time (8.3).

## 17.6 Ending a Delegation

A delegation ends:

- at `term.endsAt` (reason `expiry`), or at the age of majority where `term.atMajority` is true (reason `majority`);
- when the Authorization Service records that the basis has ended (reason `basis-ended`);
- when the delegate relinquishes it, through a Management Client (reason `relinquished`); or
- where its basis is `appointed`, when the Context Subject ends it, through a Management Client (reason `ended-by-subject`).

When a delegation ends:

- Every grant authorized under it ends at that moment. Its `status` becomes `expired`, and the HCP Provider appends `grant.expired` with reason `delegation-ended` (8.2). Invalidation and notification follow 6.2.2--6.2.4, as for a revocation.
- The HCP Provider appends a `delegation.ended` event, and sets `ended`.
- Entries written under the delegation's grants are unaffected. They are the Context Subject's, and their provenance shows the grant under which each was written (3.5).
- No grant carries over. The Context Subject authorizes any access they want to continue.

**At the age of majority.** Where a delegation ends for the reason `majority`, the HCP Provider MUST show the person, the first time they use a Management Client afterwards, a summary of the delegated period: the delegate, the grants made, and for each the client, its Client Operator, the categories, the purposes and the period, from the disclosure summary (8.6). The summary says that the former delegate can read the record of the grants they authorized (17.7).

## 17.7 Audit Access

| Party | Reads |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| The Context Subject | The whole audit log, at any time (8.3) |
| A delegate, while the delegation holds | Every event of a grant they authorized, and of a governance operation performed for them. Not the events of grants the Context Subject authorized |
| A former delegate | The events of grants they authorized: issued, revoked, and expired, and the reads and writes made under them. Each shows the event type, time, client, Client Operator, category, and purpose. Never `entryId`, `relatedEntryId`, `disclosed`, or the `data` of a `context.*` event, and never an event in a category outside the delegation's scope |
| Anyone else | Nothing |

- A former delegate's access lasts as long as the audit log is kept (8.4). It exists because a person who consented on someone else's behalf stays accountable for what they approved.
- The Context Subject cannot end a former delegate's access, and MUST be told that it exists, in the summary at the age of majority and wherever their audit log is presented.
- A delegate's or former delegate's read of the log is a governance operation (5.4), performed through a Management Client acting for them.

## 17.8 Grants Carried Over from v1

v1 required `grantor` to be recorded where the party authorizing a grant was not its subject. An HCP Provider that continues such a grant under v2 converts it:

- A delegation is created with `delegate` set from `grantor`, `basis.method` `unspecified`, `scope` set to the categories and actions of the grants converted, and `term.endsAt` null unless the provider knows an end.
- Each grant is converted with `authorizingPrincipal` identifying the delegate and naming that delegation. Its expiry is unchanged.
- The converted delegation authorizes no new grant. A new grant needs a delegation established under 17.2.
- The Context Subject is shown each converted delegation and its grants, with its basis as unspecified, through a Management Client.
- Nothing is upgraded: an `unspecified` basis remains so until a new delegation replaces it (3.11, invariant 15).

## 17.9 Why in the Protocol (Informative)

Learning Economy Foundation reviewers observed during v1 review that guardian consent can be handled in the wallet or holder application rather than in the protocol between them, and for v1 that is what was required. What the application layer cannot do is make a guardian-approved act legible in the person's own audit log, or carry the relationship when a person moves from one HCP instance to another. Those two properties are why v2 takes the question up rather than leaving it at the application layer permanently.
