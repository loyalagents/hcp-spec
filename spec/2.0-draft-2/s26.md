# 26. Roadmap

This section is written for all audiences, not only implementers. Pilot partners, policymakers, and anyone deciding whether HCP fits their use case need to know what remains open without reading the protocol sections. It is plain language, not requirements language. Each item below names where in this specification it will land, so that the roadmap points at a Part rather than a gap.

## 26.5 Notes Carried from v1

**Conflict reconciliation.** Core requires that conflicts be detected and surfaced, and lets the Context Subject resolve them (4.7). Automated reconciliation --- merging, confidence-weighted resolution, deprecation of stale inferences --- has real consequences for trust, and getting it wrong would undermine the principle that the person owns their context. It will be defined from pilot evidence on how often conflicts arise and how people resolve them. Learning Economy Foundation reviewers noted that an inference is judged by what it was drawn from, and that HCP records a confidence value without recording its basis. A structured record of inference context belongs with reconciliation, because the evidence a person needs to judge an inference is the evidence an automated model would weigh. A registered dimension vocabulary is a candidate alongside it; the values pilots adopt are better evidence for it than anything specifiable in advance (21.2).

**Category vocabulary.** The nine categories (4.5) are a starting point drawn from the Global Memory Workshop, not a validated registry. Known gaps include civic and public services context; a richer financial context for financial services pilots; cultural and linguistic context, which cuts across other categories; and research and professional credentialing. Learning Economy Foundation reviewers noted that `education_learner_profile` collapses mastery, learning history, and accommodations, which carry different sensitivity, and that family context may warrant its own category rather than sitting in `coordination_context`. Because categories are what grants are scoped to, each of these is a consent question before it is a taxonomy question. Cross-sector SME engagement and pilot evidence are the planned inputs. Richard Ng (Native) asked whether categories should be discovered rather than fixed in advance, and whether an entry could sit in several. His example is a statement that the person does not want to apply to tobacco companies, which belongs to `values_and_ethics` and to `work_context` alike. The client writing it may not know that a job-search agent reads `work_context`, so the choice of category has stakes the writer cannot see. Several categories per entry would change what a grant reaches, so that question is a consent question first.

**Credential format.** v1 permitted two securing mechanisms as a deliberate deferral (14.5). The wider identity ecosystem has moved toward selective-disclosure formats --- the EU Digital Identity wallet toward SD-JWT VC, ISO mDL toward mdoc --- in part because of the complexity of RDF canonicalization. Turn.io proposed profiling v1's Tier 2 on SD-JWT VC outright. That was not adopted, because SD-JWT VC is a distinct credential data model rather than a proof format, and adopting it would break the common envelope and the Open Badges alignment; permitting VC-JOSE-COSE obtains the canonicalization-free path and selective disclosure without either consequence. Draft 2 makes `eddsa-rdfc-2022` mandatory to verify, because it is what Open Badges verifiers already support, and keeps the others as options (14.5). Pilot evidence on which mechanisms were issued and verified, whether selective disclosure was exercised (14.9), and wallet and verifier support will inform whether v2 adds a second mandatory mechanism.

**End-to-end encrypted storage.** Relevance ordering (7.3), conflict detection (15), and server-side field assignment (5.2.3) assume the provider can read entry content. An implementation in which the operator holds only ciphertext cannot meet the first two as written. The leading resolution is to evaluate these requirements at the interface boundary, so that they are met if they occur anywhere in the Context Subject's trust domain, with a discovery declaration, `storage_encryption` (`e2e` or `server`), making the posture explicit and testable (19). v2 adopts the interface boundary as a principle (Part I); applying it to these requirements reshapes conformance, and the passages it touches (7, 15, 5.2.3, 6.1.5.1, 18, 20) are better revised together, with pilot evidence. No pilot is known to require end-to-end encrypted storage. If one does, it should say so (21.2).

**Federation.** Learning Economy Foundation reviewers pointed to wallet-attached storage, which lets a person hold and share artifacts through a storage provider of their choosing. Whether an HCP instance can be one such store, or whether the two compose some other way, is an input to the federation design.

**What HCP does not prevent.** HCP limits what context a model receives and what it may write, and holds clients accountable for downstream use. It does not constrain a model's other capabilities. A general-purpose model with access to tools may take destructive actions whether or not its client conforms to HCP. The Security and Privacy Considerations section will say so.
