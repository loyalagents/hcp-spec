# 6. Authorization

## 6.1 Grant Model

HCP specifies authorization as category-scoped, revocable grants, represented per 6.1.5. Every HCP Client MUST hold such a grant before any operation on entries is permitted. This applies without distinction to HCP Clients operated by the Provider Operator and to HCP Clients operated by others.

How a grant is obtained is defined by the authorization profile an implementation claims: the OAuth Interop Profile (13) or the First-Party Authorization Profile (12). The choice of profile does not relax any grant semantics specified in this section. Where grants are obtained through a channel other than OAuth 2.0, the requirements of 6.1.6 apply in full.

### 6.1.1 Scope Naming Convention

Scopes map directly to HCP categories and permitted actions:

    hcp:{category}:{action}

Examples:

- `hcp:health_context:read` --- read-only access to health context entries
- `hcp:general_preferences:read hcp:general_preferences:write` --- read and write access to general preferences
- `hcp:education_learner_profile:read` --- read-only access to learner profile

Wildcards are not permitted. Each scope MUST be explicitly named.

Scope strings are a compact view of a grant's `access` (6.1.5): each names a registered category and an action that a detail in `access` covers. A custom category (4.5) is a URI, and its colons would make a scope string ambiguous, so custom categories appear only in `access`, never in a scope string.

### 6.1.2 How Grants Are Obtained

The grant types available, and the authorization requests that start them, are defined by the authorization profiles: OAuth grant types and their requests in the OAuth Interop Profile (13); a non-OAuth authenticated channel, for first-party access only, in the First-Party Authorization Profile (12). Token lifetimes are defined by the OAuth Interop Profile (13).

The term "session" is not used normatively in this specification. Temporary grants are bounded by an absolute expiry timestamp, not by an implementation-defined session lifetime.

### 6.1.3 Grant Duration

| Bound | Default | Maximum |
|------------------------------------|------------|------------|
| Temporary grant absolute expiry | 4 hours | 24 hours |

Persistent grants have no fixed expiry and remain active until revoked (6.1.4).

### 6.1.4 Grant Distinction: Temporary vs. Persistent

Every grant is classified as either temporary or persistent. Temporary is the default. Persistent access requires explicit, affirmative action by the Authorizing Principal at authorization time.

**Temporary grants (default):**

- Carry an absolute expiry timestamp fixed at authorization time. The expiry MUST NOT exceed 24 hours from issuance.
- The expiry MUST be recorded in the `grant.issued` audit event, and MUST be presented to the Authorizing Principal at authorization time as a concrete time or duration.
- Refresh tokens MAY be issued within the grant's absolute expiry window. A refresh token MUST NOT be honored after the grant's absolute expiry, regardless of the refresh token's own stated lifetime.
- Expire automatically; no action by the Authorizing Principal is required to terminate. The HCP Provider MUST append a `grant.expired` event per 8.1. This does not require a background process running at the moment of expiry: per 8.1, the event may be appended on the first access presenting the expired grant, or within 24 hours of the expiry timestamp, whichever comes first. An implementation whose store is dormant between interactions may therefore write the event when it next handles a request, and is not required to run a scheduled sweep.

**Persistent grants:**

- Remain active across interactions until explicitly revoked by the Authorizing Principal
- Issue refresh tokens per the lifetimes in the OAuth Interop Profile (13)
- MUST require a distinct, affirmative action by the Authorizing Principal to elect, separate from the standard authorization confirmation

**Conformance requirement:**

Temporary access MUST be the default outcome of any authorization interaction. Electing persistent access MUST require a separate affirmative act by the Authorizing Principal, subject to the following:

- The act MUST be distinct from the act by which the Authorizing Principal confirms the grant itself. A single act MUST NOT both authorize access and elect persistence.
- Persistence MUST NOT be pre-selected by any means, including a pre-set control, a pre-filled value, or a default the person must act to decline.
- Before the act, the Authorizing Principal MUST be told what persistent access means: that it survives the current interaction and remains in effect until they revoke it.
- The elected grant type, and the modality through which it was elected, MUST be recorded in the `grant.issued` audit event.

These requirements are stated in terms of what the Authorizing Principal does and what is recorded, not in terms of interface elements. They apply identically to graphical, voice, messaging, and LLM-mediated authorization. The authorization interaction patterns (24) illustrate patterns that satisfy them in each modality and are non-normative.

### 6.1.5 Grant Representation

6.1.1 through 6.1.4 specify how authorization is bounded. This section specifies the record of an authorization: the artifact the Authorizing Principal inspects, and the object a conformance suite examines.

HCP defers to OAuth 2.0 for the authorization mechanism (1.5, 13). OAuth does not define a person-facing enumeration of granted authorizations, so the requirement that the Authorizing Principal can view and revoke all active grants (18) is not satisfied by that dependency. The representation below is the minimum sufficient to satisfy it. Where later OAuth grant-management work provides an equivalent representation, HCP is expected to map onto it rather than maintain a parallel model; field names here are chosen with that in mind.

**Grant object**

    {
      "grantId": "urn:uuid:{grant-uuid}",
      "subject": { "id": "{per-client pseudonym}" },
      "authorizingPrincipal": { "id": "{per-client pseudonym}", "delegation": "{delegation id | omitted}" },
      "client": {
        "id": "{client-identifier}",
        "name": "{name the person can recognize}",
        "operator": { "id": "{operator-identifier}", "name": "{organization name}" },
        "legalRetention": { "record": "{the record kept}", "obligation": "{the law or rule}", "period": "{how long}" }
      },
      "requestingParty": { "type": "person | organization", "id": "{identifier}", "name": "{name}" },
      "access": [
        {
          "type": "https://hcp.example/spec/authorization/context-access",
          "actions": ["read | write"],
          "categories": ["{category}"],
          "purposes": [ { "code": "{purpose URI}", "description": "{shown to the person}" } ],
          "narrowedTo": { }
        }
      ],
      "scopes": ["hcp:{category}:{action}"],
      "grantType": "temporary | persistent",
      "issuedAt": "{ISO-8601}",
      "expiresAt": "{ISO-8601 | null}",
      "status": "active | revoked | expired"
    }

| Field | Notes |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `grantId` | Stable for the life of the grant. Matches the `grantId` recorded in audit events (8.2). |
| `subject`, `authorizingPrincipal`, `client.operator`, `requestingParty` | The actors the grant binds. Their rules are in 3.4. |
| `client.name` | MUST be a name the person can recognize. A grant list presenting only opaque client identifiers does not satisfy 18. |
| `client.legalRetention` | Present only where the client declared at registration that the law requires it to keep what it receives in a record (6.2.5) |
| `access` | What the grant authorizes: one or more authorization details, each naming `actions`, `categories`, and the `purposes` the person approved for them (6.1.5.2), and optionally `narrowedTo` (6.1.5.1). The type identifies the HCP authorization detail; under the OAuth Interop Profile it is an RFC 9396 authorization detail (13.3). |
| `scopes` | Derived from `access`, in the form specified in 6.1.1, for display and for clients that use scope strings. It MUST NOT grant anything `access` does not. |
| `grantType` | Per 6.1.4. |
| `expiresAt` | For temporary grants, the absolute expiry required by 6.1.4; MUST NOT be null. For persistent grants, null. |
| `status` | `expired` is set on lapse of `expiresAt`; `revoked` on revocation by the Authorizing Principal (6.2.1). |

`authorizingPrincipal` replaces v1's `grantor`. In Core the Authorizing Principal is the Context Subject (3.3). `delegation` is present only for a grant a delegate authorized under the Delegated Authorization Profile (17.4).

**Grant access**

An HCP Provider MUST NOT disclose a grant issued to one HCP Client to any other HCP Client. This holds on every binding.

Grants are enumerated in two ways:

- **By a Management Client** acting for the Authorizing Principal, as a governance operation (5.4): all grants for the instance, filtered by status where asked; the default is active.
- **By an HCP Client reading its own grant** (5.2.6): only the grant under which that client acts.

Where no binding the implementation exposes carries these operations, the HCP Provider MUST still maintain grant objects conforming to this section, and the Authorizing Principal MUST still be able to enumerate and revoke grants through a supported modality (18). No audit event is required for reading a grant list: a person reading their own grant list is not an access to entry content, and a client reading its own grant discloses nothing it was not already told at authorization time.

**Fields deliberately excluded**

- *Last access time.* The audit log already answers when a client read what. Duplicating it into the grant object creates two records that can disagree.
- *Delegation chains.* Not permitted in this draft (17.1, 26.1).

### 6.1.5.1 Narrowed Grants

A detail in a grant's `access` MAY narrow its categories below category grain with the optional `narrowedTo` member.

    {
      "type": "https://hcp.example/spec/authorization/context-access",
      "actions": ["read"],
      "categories": ["health_context"],
      "purposes": [ { "code": "https://purposes.example/medication-reminders", "description": "Medication reminders" } ],
      "narrowedTo": { "entryIds": ["urn:uuid:...", "urn:uuid:..."] }
    }

The contents of `narrowedTo` are implementation-defined. This specification fixes what a restriction means and guarantees, not a closed vocabulary of narrowing keys: pilots will discover which dimensions matter (entry identifiers, document facets, and others), and a premature vocabulary would be wrong in the same way a premature dimension vocabulary would be (4.2). A common set is registry work (26). A custom narrowing key is a URI (25.3). An implementation narrowing by facet might write `"narrowedTo": { "documentType": ["prescription"] }`; both forms are conformant and both are legible to the person as less than the whole category.

**Four rules govern the field. They are normative and independent of storage architecture.**

- **Rule 1 --- restrictions only narrow.** A restriction MUST NOT grant access beyond the categories and actions of the detail that carries it. The effective authority of a detail is the intersection of its categories, actions, and `narrowedTo`, never the union. A `narrowedTo` naming an entry outside the detail's categories has no widening effect and MUST be ignored for authorization.
- **Rule 2 --- unknown narrowing fails closed.** A narrowing key the Authorization Service does not support MUST be rejected at issuance with invalid request (5.5). An HCP Provider holding a detail whose `narrowedTo` it cannot enforce MUST treat that detail as reaching no entry. A restriction is never ignored because it is not understood.
- **Rule 3 --- the object never overstates.** Where a grant is narrowed, the detail MUST carry its `narrowedTo`, and any person-facing presentation of the grant (18) MUST reflect the narrowing rather than the bare scope. An implementation MUST NOT present `hcp:health_context:read` as the grant when the authorization was restricted to two entries. A detail with no `narrowedTo` member is unrestricted at category grain; the member is additive, and its absence means full category.
- **Rule 4 --- enforcement.** Where the HCP Provider performs query-time filtering, a response MUST exclude any entry that the grant's restrictions place outside effective authority, on every read path: query and get entry, whatever the entry's content. Restrictions compose with minimization (7) as a prior filter: restrictions bound what the grant reaches, and minimization bounds what a query returns within that reach. An entry excluded by restriction is never a candidate for minimization.

The `grant.issued` audit event (8.1) MUST record the narrowing alongside the categories and actions, so the Context Subject's own log shows how narrow a grant was, not merely that it was issued. No new event type is required; the existing `grant.issued` detail carries it.

Rule 4 is written for server-readable storage, where the HCP Provider can filter at query time. Under end-to-end encrypted storage the provider cannot read entry content, and the enforcement site moves into the Context Subject's trust domain. That question remains open (2.4, 26); when it is resolved, Rule 4 will be revised alongside the other passages that assume provider-readable storage (4.7, 7, 5.2.3). Rules 1 through 3 govern the grant object and are unaffected.

### 6.1.5.2 Purposes

A purpose is a code, an absolute URI, with a description the person can read. This specification registers no purpose codes. Codes defined elsewhere, such as those of the Personal Data Portability Protocol (1.6), MAY be used.

- Every detail in a grant's `access` MUST carry at least one purpose. A grant is never issued without one.
- Before the approving act, the Authorizing Principal MUST be shown the description of each purpose (6.1.6).
- Where a grant request names no purpose, the Authorization Service MUST ask the Authorizing Principal to choose one or more before approving. The HCP Client learns the purposes approved by reading its own grant (5.2.6).
- Every query and get entry names a purpose code (5.2.1, 5.2.2). Where that code is not among the purposes of a detail in the grant covering the categories requested, the request MUST fail with not authorized (5.5).
- The purposes of an existing grant MUST NOT be widened without a further affirmative act (6.1.6).

What a purpose binds is the client's declaration. The HCP Provider cannot observe what a client does with what it receives. A purpose fixes what the client may say it is doing, and what the Authorizing Principal approved, and the audit log records both (8). Use outside the declared purpose remains the client's responsibility (3.11, invariant 14).

### 6.1.6 Authorization Is Distinct From Authentication

Authorization MUST NOT be inferred from authentication. The existence of an authenticated session, continued use of an application, account creation, or acceptance of terms of service MUST NOT be treated as the granting of access to entries.

Where a redirect-based OAuth flow is used, this separation is structural: the Authorizing Principal leaves the client, authorizes, and returns. Where grants are obtained through another authenticated channel --- an in-application session, a messaging interface, an LLM-mediated dialogue --- the separation MUST be preserved explicitly:

- The Authorizing Principal MUST perform an affirmative act to create a grant, distinct from any act by which they authenticated.
- Before that act, the Authorizing Principal MUST be told which categories are requested, which actions are permitted on them, the purposes for which they are requested (6.1.5.2), the longest time a disclosed copy may be used (7.6), whether the grant is temporary or persistent, when it expires, the HCP Client and its Client Operator (3.2.8), the Requesting Party where it differs from the Context Subject (3.4), and, where the client declared one, the record the law requires it to keep and that revoking will not remove what it has kept there (6.2.5). Where a standing rule excludes a requested category, they MUST be told so (6.3).
- The act MUST be recorded as a `grant.issued` event, carrying in detail the modality through which it was obtained.
- The categories, actions, or purposes of an existing grant MUST NOT be widened without a further affirmative act meeting the same requirements. A client requiring additional categories obtains a new grant or replaces the existing one.

These requirements are the substance of what a redirect-based flow accomplishes structurally. An implementation that satisfies them through a different channel satisfies this section; an implementation that does not is non-conformant regardless of binding.

### 6.1.7 Grant Request

The request that starts a grant is defined by the authorization profile (12, 13). Whatever the profile, the outcome required by 6.1.6 holds: before the Authorizing Principal acts, they MUST be told what 6.1.6 lists, and the act MUST be recorded as a `grant.issued` event.

An HCP Client requesting scopes beyond what its task requires does not violate this section --- HCP does not evaluate the necessity of a request, only what happens to it. Overbroad requests are visible to the Authorizing Principal at authorization time and to the Provider Operator in the resulting grant (6.1.5); minimization (7) governs what a granted client subsequently retrieves, not what it may ask to be granted.

If the Authorizing Principal declines the request, no grant is created. Declining is not itself a security-relevant event: this specification does not require it to be logged, and an implementation MAY choose to record it for its own purposes.

### 6.1.8 Client Attribution and Authority

A grant authorizes an HCP Client, not any component that constructs a request on its behalf. The rules that follow from this are actor invariants 5, 6, 9, 10, 14, and 15 (3.11), and apply to every operation in this section.

### 6.1.9 Governance Operations

Governance operations (5.4) MUST be performed only by a Management Client acting for the Authorizing Principal (3.2.3). Any other caller MUST receive not authorized (5.5). A grant held by a Context Client never authorizes a governance operation, whatever its scopes. Under the Delegated Authorization Profile, a Management Client may act for the Context Subject or for a delegate, within what 17 allows each.

## 6.2 Revocation

Revocation is a first-class operation.

### 6.2.1 Revocation by the Authorizing Principal

The Authorizing Principal MUST be able to revoke any grant at any time, through a Management Client (5.4) or the person-facing capabilities required by 18. Under the Delegated Authorization Profile, the Context Subject can also revoke every grant made for them, whoever authorized it (17.5).

An HCP Client MAY also give up the grant under which it acts, as a context operation (5.2.7). It cannot revoke any other grant.

Revocation MUST NOT be rate limited. The Authorizing Principal's ability to revoke access is not subject to throttling under any circumstance.

### 6.2.2 Revocation Propagation

Upon revocation:

- All authorization evidence issued under the grant, including access and refresh tokens, MUST be invalidated immediately: once revocation completes, no request presenting evidence issued under the grant succeeds. To meet this, the HCP Provider MUST determine the grant's current status on every request, by its own record of the grant or by token introspection (RFC 7662). It MUST NOT rely on a token's own validity alone, because a self-contained token stays valid after its grant is revoked.
- The revocation event MUST be appended to the audit log within 60 seconds.
- If the HCP Client holds disclosed entries, the HCP Provider MUST notify it, per 6.2.3, where the client has registered a notification endpoint.

Revocation ends the use of every copy disclosed under the grant, whatever its use-until time (7.6):

- When an HCP Client is notified that its grant is revoked (6.2.3), it MUST stop using every copy disclosed under that grant, and MUST NOT pass any of them on. The one exception is a copy kept under a declared legal obligation (6.2.5).
- A request that fails because its grant has been revoked is itself a notice. The client MUST treat it exactly as a notification.

What the client does with its copies remains the client's responsibility (3.11, invariant 14). The HCP Provider cannot enforce deletion; it bounds the harm with use-until, makes revocation reach the client, and records what happened (6.2.3, 6.2.4).

### 6.2.3 Revocation Notification

Where an HCP Client has registered a notification endpoint, the notification required by 6.2.2 is delivered to it as a Security Event Token (RFC 8417). How it is delivered --- push delivery, signing, identifiers, and retry --- is defined by the HTTP Binding (9.9).

A client with no notification endpoint still receives every disclosure, but its use-until times are capped at 1 hour (7.6).

**Auditing.** A `revocation.notified` event MUST be appended on first successful delivery, and on abandonment after retries are exhausted, with the outcome recorded in detail.

**Independence.** Revocation takes effect at the HCP Provider immediately per 6.2.2, whether or not notification succeeds. Failure or delay in delivery MUST NOT postpone or reverse the invalidation of authorization evidence.

**Abuse controls.** An HCP Provider MAY rate-limit notification delivery, and the registration of notification endpoints, to protect itself and the endpoints. It MUST NOT rate-limit, delay, or condition revocation itself (6.2.1).

### 6.2.4 When Notification Fails

Where delivery of a revocation notification is abandoned (9.9), the HCP Provider MUST tell the Authorizing Principal, through the person-facing capabilities required by 18, that the client was not notified, and the time at which the last copy disclosed under the grant stops being usable. That time is the latest `useUntil` among those copies, which the audit log records (8.2). The disclosure summary shows it as well (8.6).

### 6.2.5 Copies Kept Under a Legal Obligation

Some clients are bound by law to keep what they receive. A clinic that records a patient's history in its medical record is bound by law to keep that record for years. Revocation cannot recall it, and this section makes that known in advance rather than leaving the promise of 6.2.2 unmet.

- An HCP Client that is required by law to keep context it receives in a record MUST declare it at registration: the record, the obligation, and the period. The HCP Provider records the declaration in each grant to that client as `client.legalRetention` (6.1.5).
- Before the Authorizing Principal approves such a grant, they MUST be told that the client keeps what it receives in that record, under that obligation, and that revoking the grant will not remove it (6.1.6).
- A copy the client places in that record is exempt from use-until (7.6) and from the end of use on revocation (6.2.2) only so far as the obligation requires. Once its use-until time passes or the grant is revoked, the client keeps it to meet that obligation, does not otherwise use it under this grant, and MUST NOT pass it on under HCP.
- When the grant is revoked, the HCP Provider MUST tell the Authorizing Principal, through the person-facing capabilities required by 18, which client keeps copies under a declared obligation, in which record, and for how long. The disclosure summary shows it (8.6).
- A copy kept under an obligation the client did not declare is not covered by this section. Keeping it after revocation does not conform.

What the law requires of the record itself, such as giving the patient access to it, is outside this specification.

## 6.3 Standing Rules

A grant governs one client. A standing rule governs every grant: "never give my context to anyone in this namespace", for everything or only for health. The Context Subject sets it once, and the HCP Provider enforces it on every grant, present and future.

    {
      "ruleId": "urn:uuid:{rule-uuid}",
      "effect": "deny",
      "operators": ["{DNS domain | operator identifier}"],
      "categories": ["{category}"],
      "setBy": "{context-subject | delegation id}",
      "createdAt": "{ISO-8601}"
    }

| Member | Rule |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `ruleId` | Stable for the life of the rule |
| `effect` | `deny`, the only effect in this draft. A standing rule never widens access. |
| `operators` | The Client Operators (3.2.8) the rule applies to. A DNS domain matches every operator whose identifier's host is that domain or a subdomain of it; any other value matches one operator identifier exactly. |
| `categories` | The categories the rule applies to. Absent means every category, held now or later: the rule applies across all of the person's context. |
| `setBy` | Set by the HCP Provider: `context-subject`, or the `delegationId` of the delegate who set it (17.2) |
| `createdAt` | Set by the HCP Provider |

Requirements:

- The Context Subject sets and removes standing rules through a Management Client, as a governance operation (5.4). The HCP Provider appends `rule.set` or `rule.removed` (8.1).
- The HCP Provider MUST NOT issue a grant whose `access` reaches a category a standing rule denies to that grant's Client Operator. Before the approving act, the Authorizing Principal is told which requested categories a rule excludes (6.1.6). The rule can be overridden only by removing it, which is a separate act.
- A standing rule applies to existing grants from the moment it is set, through the status check made on every request (6.2.2). A request it covers fails with not authorized (5.5). Where it covers every category a grant reaches, the HCP Provider revokes the grant, and 6.2.2--6.2.4 apply.
- A delegate cannot authorize a grant a standing rule denies (17.4). Under the Delegated Authorization Profile, a delegate MAY also set standing rules, within the categories of the delegation's scope, through a Management Client acting for them. The Context Subject can remove any standing rule; a delegate can remove only the rules they set. Each rule records who set it, in `setBy`.
- Standing rules are part of the Context Subject's export (5.4.1). An import (5.4.3) brings them with it, because they only restrict access.

A standing rule controls which clients receive context. What a client may do with context it has received --- sign for the person, pass it to another agent within its own operator --- is a directive to the agent rather than a rule about access, and is later work (26.1).
