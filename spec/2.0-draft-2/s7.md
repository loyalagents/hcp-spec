# 7. Disclosure and Minimization

Data minimization is enforced at query time, not at grant time. An authorized category is the outer bound of what may be returned, not a description of what should be.

This section states minimization as four requirements rather than as an outcome. Requirements 7.1, 7.2, and 7.4 are mechanically verifiable; 7.3 is verified by declaration. 7.5 specifies how a response reports them, and 7.6 how long a disclosed copy may be used.

## 7.1 Category Bound

A response MUST NOT contain any entry whose `classification.category` is outside the scopes of the grant under which the request was made. This holds regardless of the relevance of the entry to the stated query.

## 7.2 Quantity Bound

A response MUST NOT contain more entries than the lesser of the requested `maxEntries` and a ceiling of 20. The ceiling applies to every binding.

An implementation MUST NOT return additional entries in order to reach `maxEntries`. Where fewer entries meet the implementation's relevance criteria than were requested, fewer are returned. `maxEntries` is a ceiling, not a target.

Query results are not paginated. `maxEntries` is a minimization control rather than a page size, and a cursor over a minimized result set would permit an authorized client to enumerate an entire category one page at a time --- precisely what this section exists to prevent. Where a pilot finds the ceiling of 20 insufficient, the appropriate response is evidence toward raising it.

The quantity bound is defeated if a Context Client issues a sequence of semantically rephrased queries to sweep a category a few entries at a time, assembling in aggregate what a single request may not return. A client that does so is acting adversarially toward the minimization contract, and an HCP Provider MAY treat a pattern of rephrased queries against one category and grant as a signal to rate-limit or to decline.

## 7.3 Relevance Ordering

An implementation MUST apply a relevance ordering to candidate entries and MUST return entries in that order. Returning entries in storage order, insertion order, or arbitrary order is non-conformant.

An implementation MUST declare the method determining the final ordering in its discovery document (19). The set of method values is open; the following are registered:

| Value | Meaning |
|------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `lexical` | Ordering derived from term or token overlap between query and entry |
| `semantic` | Ordering derived from embedding or vector similarity |
| `llm` | Ordering derived from a language model's judgment of relevance to the stated query and purpose |
| `none` | No ordering applied, because the request named a single entry (5.2.2) |

Where an implementation combines methods, it declares the one determining the final ordering.

## 7.4 Disclosures to Context Clients

Where the requester is a Context Client, a response MUST carry each entry as a **disclosure**, never as the stored entry:

    {
      "ref": "{grant-scoped reference}",
      "version": 2,
      "content": { },
      "evidence": [ ],
      "assertion": { "declarationType": "...", "confidence": null, "validUntil": null },
      "classification": { "category": "{category}", "dimension": "{dimension | null}" },
      "standing": { "assurance": "unverified | authenticated | cryptographically-verified", "confirmed": true },
      "useUntil": "{ISO-8601}",
      "relationships": [ { "type": "{type}", "target": "{ref}" } ]
    }

| Member | Rule |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `ref` | A reference to the entry, opaque to the client, stable for one grant and one entry, and different under every other grant. It MUST NOT reveal or be derivable into the entry's `id`. The client names the entry by its `ref` in get entry, revise, and withdraw (5.2). A `ref` presented under any other grant fails with not authorized (5.5). |
| `version`, `content`, `assertion` | As stored, for the version disclosed (4.2) |
| `evidence` | As stored, including each item's `verification` (4.2.2), except that a credential may be disclosed in derived form. An item carrying a derived credential adds `revealed`, the JSON Pointers into the stored credential of the claims it reveals (14.9). |
| `classification` | The category and dimension only |
| `standing` | `assurance` is `record.provenance.assertedBy.assurance`; `confirmed` is true where the most recent confirmation record on the version disclosed is `confirmed` (3.8) |
| `useUntil` | The time after which the client may no longer use the copy (7.6) |
| `relationships` | Only relationships whose target is disclosed in the same response, with the target named by its `ref` (4.2.3) |

Everything else in the entry is withheld: its `id` and `subject`, its status, and the rest of `record`, including the provenance fields that identify parties (`source`, `submittedByClient`, `authorizedUnder`, `assertedBy.id`, `confirmedBy`), `record.conversion`, and `record.metadata`. These fields describe the Context Subject's activity with other systems rather than the entry itself. Disclosing them tells one client which other clients a person uses and which of their conversations produced what, which no grant authorizes. A stable `id` would also let two clients that compare notes recognize the same entry, which a grant-scoped `ref` does not. What a client needs to judge an entry's standing, it receives in `standing` (3.8).

A credential in `evidence` cannot be altered without breaking its proof (4.2.2). Where it is secured with a mechanism that supports selective disclosure, the HCP Provider SHOULD disclose a derived credential revealing only the claims the request's purpose requires and those its issuer made mandatory (14.9). Otherwise it is disclosed whole, and may carry identifiers of its own, such as the credential subject's identifier.

Disclosure is a property of the response, not of storage. Entries MUST continue to carry complete provenance as stored, and the Context Subject's own access --- through export, the audit log, or a Management Client --- receives whole entries with their stable identifiers.

## 7.5 Reporting

Query and get-entry responses MUST carry a minimization report:

    "minimization": {
      "method": "{declared method, or none}"
    }

A get-entry response reports `method` as `none` (7.3). The report MUST NOT include the number of entries considered, the number available within scope, or any other measure of what was withheld; such counts disclose the size of a Context Subject's holdings to a client that was not granted them.

## 7.6 Use-Until

Every disclosure carries `useUntil`. After that time, the HCP Client MUST NOT use the copy, or pass it on, without fetching it again (3.12), except as a declared legal obligation requires (6.2.5). The HCP Provider sets it at disclosure:

1. **An explicit time applies first.** Where the entry has an `assertion.validUntil`, or its credential a `validUntil` (14.2), or the provider sets a time of its own, `useUntil` is the earliest of these. An explicit time earlier than the floor below still applies: content that no longer holds is not usable for an hour.
2. **Otherwise, a heuristic.** `useUntil` is the disclosure time plus 10% of the time since the entry was last revised, the `recordedAt` of its current version (4.2). The heuristic follows HTTP heuristic freshness (RFC 9111 §4.2.2).
3. **A floor and ceilings.** A heuristic time is never less than 1 hour after disclosure. No time is more than 30 days after disclosure, or 24 hours for a category whose default sensitivity is High (4.5). A custom category with no registered sensitivity is treated as High.
4. **No notification channel, short copies.** Where the HCP Client has no registered notification endpoint (6.2.3), `useUntil` is never more than 1 hour after disclosure. The cap comes from the missing channel, not from the grant.

The grant does not cap `useUntil`: a temporary grant limits access, not use of a copy already disclosed. Revocation does end it. When a client is notified that a grant is revoked, every copy disclosed under that grant becomes unusable at once, whatever its `useUntil` (6.2.3).

Before the Authorizing Principal approves a grant, they MUST be told the longest time a disclosed copy may be used under it: the ceiling that applies to the categories requested, or 1 hour where the client has no notification endpoint (6.1.6).

The floor and ceilings are starting values, to be tuned with pilot evidence (21.2). `useUntil` is not signed in this draft.
