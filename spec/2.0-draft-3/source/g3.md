# G3. Health Guide

This guide assembles Core, the General Context Profile (11), the Health Context Profile (16), and the VC Evidence Profile (14) for health pilots.

## G3.1 Sensitivity and Token Lifetimes

The Health Context Profile requires every entry in `health_context` to be treated as high-sensitivity regardless of content, and recommends that access tokens under a grant including `health_context` not exceed one hour (16.2).

The reason for shorter tokens is exposure. Revocation invalidates authorization evidence at the HCP Provider immediately (6.2.2), but a token that has leaked, or that a resource server cannot check against revocation at once, is accepted until it expires. A shorter lifetime narrows that window. How immediate invalidation is achieved for self-contained tokens is open (P1).

## G3.2 Documents That Arrive Before a Grant

Clinical documents often reach a deployment before the person has authorized anything. Core requires that such content produce no entry, no audit event on the Context Subject's log, and no state visible to a Context Client (4.6).

The specification does not mandate a holding mechanism, but a safe pattern is a custody area outside the HCP data model --- an operator-side inbound queue --- that holds the raw document, writes no HCP entry, exposes nothing to any HCP Client, and is drained into the instance only once the Authorizing Principal has authorized the relevant category. An item that is never authorized is never ingested, and is disposed of according to the Provider Operator's retention policy. The property to preserve is that an ungranted document is not yet part of the person's context.

## G3.3 Facets Within the Category

A health pilot may scope grants to `health_context` while distinguishing prescriptions, laboratory results, and clinical notes inside the entry, as facets. Both facets and separate categories are conformant. A narrowed grant (6.1.5.1) can restrict a grant to one facet, for example `"narrowedTo": { "documentType": ["prescription"] }`. Pilots should record which approach they took and whether people wanted finer control than it gave them (G1.2).

## G3.4 Clinical Assertions and the Person's Own Statements

Δ CHANGED (v2-draft-3) --- A clinical assertion stands against an unconfirmed inference; against the person's own statement the conflict is shown to them, not settled (M3).

A model's inference about the person's health is not disclosed to any client until the person confirms it (16.2). A deployment that infers, for example, that a patient stopped a medication writes the inference as `model-inferred` and asks the person to confirm it, as Turn.io's maternal-health pilot does.

A clinician's or clinical system's assertion is `issuer-attested` (4.4). Where it contradicts an unconfirmed inference, the inference is held back (4.7). Where it contradicts something the person has declared, neither is held back and the conflict is never settled by the provider: it is shown to the person, who decides what to do (4.4). Which should prevail is a domain judgment, and this is the case most likely to matter in health pilots. Evidence from them is the intended input to automated resolution (P1).

## G3.5 Credentials

v1 committed that VC Evidence would be required for health pilots at v2. The Health Context Profile meets it: a clinician's or clinical system's assertion carries a credential, as its content or in support, and its status is checked at least hourly (16.2, 14.7). What the person says about their own health does not need one.

- A provider claiming the profile cannot hold a clinical assertion that carries no credential. Pilots whose clinical systems do not yet issue credentials should plan for issuance early.
- Every VC Evidence implementation verifies `eddsa-rdfc-2022` (14.5). An issuer that secures credentials with VC-JOSE-COSE, which avoids RDF canonicalization, should check that the providers its pilots use declare it in `vc_securing` (19).

## G3.6 HIPAA

HIPAA compliance requirements sit outside the scope of this specification and must be addressed at the implementation layer.

## G3.7 Records, Erasure and Residency

Three questions recur in health pilots: what a clinic keeps, how data is erased, and where it lives.

- **Clinical records.** A clinic that must keep what it receives in its medical record declares that obligation at registration. The person is told before approving, and again at revocation, that revoking will not remove it (6.2.5). Declare it; an undeclared retention is not covered.
- **Erasure.** Hard deletion removes an entry for good (5.4.2). Destroying the keys under which an entry is encrypted meets it, provided the entry can no longer be recovered from any replica or backup.
- **Residency.** Where the person's context must be stored and processed in their jurisdiction, that is a property of the deployment, and nothing in this specification prevents it. Pilots should record residency requirements they met (G1.2).

For payloads inside health entries, established code systems such as HL7 FHIR resources and terminology and SNOMED CT should be considered before anything bespoke. Pilots should record which they used; they are inputs to a dimension vocabulary (P1).
