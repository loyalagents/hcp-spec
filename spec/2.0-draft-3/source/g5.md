# G5. Deployment Patterns

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- Patterns for deployments the earlier guides do not cover (Gates Foundation, Neil Hausmann; Sandy Pentland, MIT Connection Science; Dimagi, Jonathan Jackson, DM5, DM6; the Pro-Human AI Stack's principle of inclusive access).

This section is informative. Each pattern describes a setting, how the specification applies there, and what remains open.

## G5.1 Health Workers Recording for People

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- A health worker records into each person's context (Gates Foundation; Sandy Pentland).

A community health worker visits many households and records what she learns into each person's context, from one tablet.

- **One grant for each person.** Her application is a Context Client holding a separate grant from each person it serves, never one grant for all of them. Where the application is an institutional integration, it requests each token under the grant of the person it is serving (13.2).
- **Approval from the person.** The person approves the grant, not the health worker. Where the person has a phone, approval can come by a messaging reply or a short code on their own handset (G4). Where the person has no device, a spoken disclosure and confirmation in the health worker's presence is a pattern this draft does not yet specify (P1.4); the identity questions in 21.3 apply to it with particular force.
- **Whose statement it is.** What the person tells her is recorded as the person's statement, `unverified` until the person confirms it (3.7). What she observes is recorded with her as the asserter. A result the clinic stands behind is `issuer-attested`, written under an attestation authorization the clinic or the health ministry gave her application (14.10).
- **Health inferences.** An inference her application makes is held back from other clients until the person confirms it (16.2).

## G5.2 Shared Devices

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- One phone in a household (Gates Foundation, Neil Hausmann).

Several people may use one phone, and in some households one phone number serves the family.

- **Each person has their own context.** An HCP instance belongs to one person. On a shared device, each person signs in separately, and a Management Client acts only for the person who authenticated (3.2.3).
- **A phone number is not a person.** Sign-in through a messaging application identifies a number, not who is holding the phone (21.3). Before a grant reaches a category of High sensitivity, the Authorization Service asks for more than that sign-in (6.1.6), such as a PIN the person alone knows.
- **Prefer temporary grants.** A temporary grant ends with the interaction (6.1.4), so a later user of the same phone does not inherit it. A deployment that offers persistent grants on shared phones explains what that means for whoever uses the phone next.
- **Copies on the device.** A client that keeps disclosed context on the phone discards it at its use-until time (7.6), and before then keeps it where another user of the phone cannot read it.

## G5.3 Low Connectivity

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- People and health workers who are often offline (Gates Foundation, Neil Hausmann).

- **Reads need the provider.** A query reaches the provider when it is made. A client working offline relies on what it was disclosed earlier, within its use-until time (7.6). A deployment that expects long offline periods sets use-until accordingly, within the floor and ceilings that apply (7.6).
- **Writes can wait.** A client can hold a write and submit it when it reconnects. The provider records when it received the entry (3.5); the time of the observation belongs in the content.
- **Revocation arrives late.** A client that is offline learns of a revocation when it reconnects, and the person is told when notification does not succeed (6.2.4).
- **Encrypted storage.** Where the component that reads the context runs on a phone that is often out of coverage, queries fail until it returns (G1.4).
- **Approval over thin channels.** The Device Authorization Grant (13.2), a messaging reply, or a keypad entry on a basic handset (G4) need less bandwidth than a browser redirect.

## G5.4 Mediated Access Where Data Stays Put

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- Records that stay with an institution (Gates Foundation, Neil Hausmann; Sandy Pentland).

Some records cannot leave the institution that holds them, by law or by policy: a national health record, a school system's records.

- **The institution as provider.** The institution can operate an HCP Provider over the records it holds (3.2.4). The records stay where they are; the person approves each grant, clients receive only what a grant covers, reduced to what the request needs (7), and every access is in the person's log (8).
- **Several providers for one person.** A person may then have context with more than one provider: the institution's, and one of their own choosing. HCP does not yet let one provider act as a client of another on the person's behalf; that is the cross-HCP federation item (P1.4).
- **Processing in place.** Where the institution runs a model over the records itself and releases only results, the results are entries the institution asserts, with the records as their source (3.5).

## G5.5 The Workplace

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- An employer that runs a provider for its staff (Dimagi, DM5, DM6; GliaNet on the workplace).

An organization with many agents and many staff may hold context about its own staff, so that its agents can draw on it.

- **The employee is still the Context Subject.** Each member of staff approves the grants on their context, can read their log, and can withdraw access (6, 8). The employer, as Provider Operator, does not approve grants on their behalf.
- **The imbalance of power.** Approval given to an employer is not freely given in the way approval given to an assistant one chose is. A deployment can limit what it asks for to `work_context`, keep a person's own context apart, and say plainly what happens if they decline. Unless storage is end-to-end encrypted, the operator can read what it stores (4.8).
- **Between the employer's own agents.** Within one operator, HCP allows a client to pass context to the operator's own components (3.12). What a person tells one agent can therefore reach others unless the deployment prevents it. A deployment that wants what is said to one agent to stay there gives each agent its own client and grant, records each statement with the agent that heard it, and lets the person set a directive that forbids passing on (6.4).
- **Leaving.** When a member of staff leaves, they can export their context (5.4.1). What the employer itself attested about them, such as a completed training, travels as an attested entry; what the employer may keep is a matter for its legal obligations (6.2.5).

## G5.6 Inclusive Access

Δ NEW (v2-draft-3) --- Reaching people who are not on a smartphone and a fast connection (the Pro-Human AI Stack, inclusive access).

- **Every modality.** Approval works by voice, by messaging and by keypad, as well as on a screen (G4). A person who cannot use one is offered another.
- **Language and literacy.** What the person is told before approving (6.1.6) is given in a language they read or hear comfortably, and in plain words. A disclosure that is accurate but not understood does not inform anyone.
- **Help without handing over control.** Someone helping a person, such as a relative or a health worker, can read the screen aloud; the approval remains the person's own act. Where another person is to decide for them, that is a delegation (17), recorded as one.
- **Cost.** Data costs money. Approval and queries that need little bandwidth (G5.3) keep HCP usable where data is expensive.
